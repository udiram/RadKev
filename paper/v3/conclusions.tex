\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

Specializing an open, general-purpose decision model on public radiology data improved its accuracy on a radiology benchmark: the specialized 27B model was more accurate than its starting point, a hosted general-purpose decision model and LLMs of the same size, and did not differ detectably from its own backbone with reasoning enabled, at a fraction of its latency, and the specialized 9B model did not differ detectably from a general-purpose model three times its size. % [C1] [C4] [C5] [C9] [C19] [C23]
The gains were concentrated in a few tasks and, for case diagnosis, reflected recognition of the answer options rather than reading of the case; the frequency of answers in the training data shaped the models' default on a catch-all option, and hedged findings, rare in the training data, remained a weakness on reports from another institution, where the 27B model lost accuracy. % [C3] [C18] [C22]
For categorical radiology decisions that must run within an institution, a specialized open decision model is a practical alternative to larger general-purpose models and to LLMs, provided that its training answers and answer spaces are curated for the intended use and that it is validated on local data.
