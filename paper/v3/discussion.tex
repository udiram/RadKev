\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Specializing a general-purpose decision model on public radiology data improved its task-averaged accuracy on a radiology benchmark by \V{r3_prim_tm} percentage points, and the specialized 27B model was more accurate than its starting point, the hosted OpenAI Decisions API, MedGemma-27B-text and its own backbone used as an LLM, over the benchmark as a whole and over the human-assigned tasks alone. It was also more accurate than that backbone with reasoning enabled, at \V{r3_lt_ratio_think}-fold lower latency. % [C1] [C4] [C5] [C16] [C19] [C23]
Before specialization, the backbone was about as accurate when queried as a general-purpose decision model as when queried as an LLM, so the advantage arose from specialization rather than from the decision format alone. % [C6]
At 9B, specialization raised the task mean by \V{r3_spec9_tm} points, and the specialized model did not differ detectably from a general-purpose model three times its size; at 27B, the gain from specialization did not differ detectably from that from a threefold increase in model size when tasks were weighted equally, and exceeded it when the human-assigned questions were pooled. % [C8] [C9]
Analyses other than the primary comparison are exploratory.

The gains were concentrated in a few tasks: they were largest in the detection of errors in reports and in Eurorad diagnosis, absent in finding presence and subspecialty classification, and larger on tasks from sources whose training splits were used for specialization (\V{r3_prim_trained_tm} points) than on tasks from sources used only for evaluation (\V{r3_prim_evalonly_tm} points). The options-only control qualifies the Eurorad gain: without the case, the specialized models still selected the correct diagnosis in most questions, and their gain over the general-purpose models was larger than with the case, so this gain reflects recognition of the case author's final diagnosis among the options rather than reading of the case. The same caution applies to other benchmarks whose incorrect options are written by the authors of the correct answer. % [C18]
For RadKev-27B, part of the gain also depended on familiar question phrasing (\V{r3_wd_held} points on withheld and \V{r3_wd_seen} on seen instruction wordings), whereas for RadKev-9B it did not (\V{r3_wd9_held} and \V{r3_wd9_seen} points). % [C17]
Removing the imaging findings reduced the accuracy of every system on the subspecialty question, so classifying an interpreted case, as posed in the benchmark, is easier than routing a study before it is read. % [C13]

Two results concern the answers in the training data. On the RadCases panel question, the catch-all option that no topic applies was the most frequent training answer (\V{r3_rc_train_none_pct}\%), and the specialized models adopted it as their default (accuracy \V{r3_rcp_raw_rk27}\% for RadKev-27B) until their answer distributions were corrected for the training answer frequencies (\V{r3_t_v3_27_rcpanel}\%, against \V{r3_t_stock27_rcpanel}\% for Kev-27B). % [C3]
On radiologist-annotated reports from another institution (RadGraph-XL), specialization changed the accuracy of the 27B model by \V{r3_rg_d} points (\V{r3_rg_d_ci}) and that of the 9B model by \V{r3_rg_d9} points (\V{r3_rg_d9_ci}). Before and after specialization, the decision models frequently classified hedged findings as present (correct in \V{r3_rgu_stock27}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_rgu_v3_27}\% for RadKev-27B, against \V{r3_rgu_qwen38}\% for Qwen3.8-27B); such findings formed \V{r3_rgu_share}\% of these questions but only \V{r3_rgu_train_share}\% of the status answers in the training data, and specialization did not correct this tendency. The gains on the benchmark therefore do not carry over unchanged to reports from another institution, and the training answers should cover the answer distribution of the intended use. % [C22]

Beyond accuracy, a decision model returns a probability for every admissible answer of every question in one pass, which permits automation of confident decisions and referral of uncertain ones \citep{geifman2017selective}: at a 5\% error budget, RadKev-27B could answer \V{r3_cal_v3_27_cov}\% of the human-assigned questions, compared with \V{r3_cal_stock27_cov}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_cal_qwen38_cov}\% for Qwen3.8-27B. Its expected calibration error was similar to that of Kev-27B, but it made more confident errors (\V{r3_cal_v3_27_ce}\% vs \V{r3_cal_stock27_ce}\% of questions), so its temperature and any automation thresholds should be set on local data \citep{guo2017calibration}. % [C11] [C12] [C15]
Per question, an LLM scored from its option-letter logits was about as fast as the decision model (\V{r3_lt_q} vs \V{r3_lt_rk27} ms), and with 255 options an LLM generating the number of its choice was faster (\V{r3_as_lat_q_255} vs \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_255} ms); the latency advantage applies to LLMs that generate their answers, above all with reasoning (\V{r3_lt_qthink} s), and to records with several questions, which a decision model answers in one pass. % [C21] [C19]

These results agree with earlier work on domain adaptation, in which continued training on domain text improved downstream performance \citep{gururangan2020dontstop} and lightweight adaptation of open models has been proposed for clinical and radiology text \citep{vanveen2024adapted,huisman2025lightweight,bluethgen2025bestpractices,savage2025opensource}, although prompting alone has also allowed a generalist model to exceed medically fine-tuned models \citep{nori2023medprompt}. For decision models, which have been less accurate than frontier LLMs on medical questions (Section~\ref{sec:intro_spec}), the present results show that specialization can make an open model more accurate than LLMs of the same size on radiology decisions. LLMs have been evaluated for the detection of errors in reports \citep{gertz2024errors} and for selecting imaging under the ACR Appropriateness Criteria \citep{rau2023accgpt,yao2025radcases}; specialization on several thousand labeled reports improved error detection substantially. % [C5] [C16]

\subsection{Clinical implementation}\label{sec:disc_clinical}

\paragraph{Suitable decisions.} None of the uses below was evaluated in this study. A decision model addresses the categorical decisions among the roles proposed for language models in radiology \citep{paschali2025foundation,bluethgen2025bestpractices} and complements, rather than replaces, generative systems. It suits frequent decisions made on radiology text whose admissible answers can be listed in advance: examination selection from an order's indication, prioritization of requests and worklists, routing to subspecialty readers, detection of critical or incidental findings and of report errors, extraction of follow-up recommendations, and consistency checks between findings and impression. A single request can populate several fields of a worklist, with confident decisions automated and uncertain ones referred for review. It is less suited to decisions whose answers cannot be listed in advance, to generating text and to decisions that depend on the images.

\paragraph{Resources, cost and latency.} All components are openly available and run on institutional hardware, so patient data need not leave the institution; the RadKev weights are licensed for non-commercial research use. A hosted decision model is inexpensive to query, as the benchmark evaluation of the OpenAI Decisions API, which cost US\$\V{r3_od_cost}, illustrates, but every request sends the report text outside the institution; a locally hosted open-weight model provides the same form of decision without this transfer. Inference used two 48 GB GPUs for the 27B models and one for the 9B models, and specialization took \V{r3_hours_27} h (RadKev-27B) and \V{r3_hours_9} h (RadKev-9B) on four such GPUs, so retraining on local data is feasible on a single workstation; the 9B model trades a lower hardware requirement for a moderate loss of accuracy (\V{r3_a_v3_9_tm}\% vs \V{r3_a_v3_27_tm}\%). At the measured latencies (\V{r3_lt_rk27} and \V{r3_lt_rk9} ms per question), one GPU pair answers about \V{r3_thr_rk27_hr} questions per hour without batching (\V{r3_thr_rk9_hr} for the 9B model on one GPU), an extrapolation that assumes uninterrupted sequential requests. Deployment would further require integration with the radiology information system, a labeled local sample on which to measure accuracy and refit the temperature, and monitoring over time.

\paragraph{Answer spaces and safeguards.} A decision model can only choose among the answers it is offered, and the composition of the answer space affected accuracy more than its size (Section~\ref{sec:res_answerspace}). For closed decisions, such as the examination, protocol, priority or follow-up category, the answer space can be taken from the institution's own catalogs, triage scales or structured-reporting templates, which are reviewed, versioned and audited. For open decisions such as differential diagnosis, candidates can be retrieved from a curated catalog by similarity to the case or written by an LLM, and the decision model used to choose among them with a calibrated probability; lists of tens rather than hundreds of options keep latency low. Catch-all options should be curated together with the training answers, because their training frequency determines how often a fine-tuned model selects them. Before deployment, the inclusion rate, the proportion of local cases whose correct answer is among the candidates, should be measured, the temperature refitted on local cases and answer spaces, and automation thresholds set locally; decisions with clinical consequences should remain subject to review by a radiologist \citep{palmer2026kev}. % [C3]

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The test splits of Eurorad, RadCases, ReXErr, MedMCQA and MedQA come from the same collections as their training splits, and ReXErr's errors were inserted by an LLM. Apart from the sources used only for evaluation, whose questions follow the formats of the training tasks, the only external test set was RadGraph-XL, on which specialization lowered the accuracy of the 27B model. The correction of the RadCases panel distributions for the training answer frequencies was chosen after the results were known. RSNA-RadioQA comprises \V{r3_n_rsna} questions and several knowledge tasks retain few questions after the radiology filter, so their estimates are imprecise, and the IU/Open-i chest radiograph reports dominate any average over questions. The questions on imaging orders, triage and follow-up were labeled by agreement of two LLMs without validation against human judgment \citep{pangakis2023validation}, and the subspecialty question, posed with the imaging findings, measures the classification of an interpreted case rather than routing. % [C13]
All data were public, the models were not validated on institutional reports, and accuracy was not examined across patient subgroups. The LLMs were evaluated zero-shot with a single prompt, MedGemma-27B-text without reasoning in the main evaluation, and no LLM was fine-tuned on the same data, so the effect of the decision format is not separated from that of domain fine-tuning. Every model was trained once, with a single random seed; public examination questions and Eurorad cases may have been part of the pretraining data of every model, so the conclusions rest on paired differences; and the analysis plan was not publicly registered. Latency was measured one request at a time, and LLMs served with batching may achieve higher throughput. All questions were posed on text.

\subsection{Future work}\label{sec:future}

The specialized model should be compared with radiologists in a reader study on institutional reports, with answer spaces taken from the institution's own catalogs, and its error-detection and appropriateness decisions tested on reports and orders from other institutions. An LLM fine-tuned on the same data would separate the effect of the decision format from that of fine-tuning, and repeated training with different seeds would quantify the variability of the gains. Methods of constructing answer spaces should be compared by their inclusion rate and the accuracy and calibration they yield, routing should be evaluated on the information available before the read, and extending the approach to images would address the larger part of radiological practice.
