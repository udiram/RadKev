\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Specializing a general-purpose decision model on public radiology data improved its task-averaged accuracy on a radiology benchmark by \V{r3_prim_tm} percentage points, and the specialized 27B model was more accurate than its starting point, the hosted decision models Jev and OpenAI Decisions and its own backbone used as an LLM, over the benchmark as a whole and over the human-labeled tasks alone. On a sample of \V{r3_rs_q} questions, it was also more accurate than that backbone with reasoning enabled, at \V{r3_lt_ratio_think}-fold lower latency. % [C1] [C4] [C16] [C19] [C23] [C26]
Before specialization, the backbone was approximately as accurate when queried as a general-purpose decision model as when queried as an LLM, so the advantage arose from specialization rather than from the decision format alone. % [C6]
Kev-27B, which reproduces the interface of Jev with open weights, was less accurate than Jev (\V{r3_jvk_tm} points), and specialization reversed this ordering. % [C26] [C27]
At 9B, specialization raised the task mean by \V{r3_spec9_tm} points, and the specialized model did not differ detectably from a general-purpose model three times its size; at 27B, the gain from specialization did not differ detectably from that from a threefold increase in model size when tasks were weighted equally, and exceeded it when the human-labeled questions were pooled. % [C8] [C9]
Analyses other than the primary comparison are exploratory.

The gains were concentrated in a few tasks: they were largest in the detection of errors in reports and in Eurorad diagnosis, absent in finding presence and subspecialty classification, and larger on tasks from sources whose training splits were used for specialization (\V{r3_prim_trained_tm} points) than on tasks from sources used only for evaluation (\V{r3_prim_evalonly_tm} points). The options-only control qualifies the Eurorad gain: without the case, the specialized models still selected the correct diagnosis in most questions, and their gain over the general-purpose models was larger than with the case, so this gain reflects recognition of the case author's final diagnosis among the options rather than reading of the case. The same caution applies to other benchmarks whose incorrect options are written by the authors of the correct answer. % [C18]
For RadKev-27B, part of the gain also depended on familiar question phrasing (\V{r3_wd_held} points on withheld and \V{r3_wd_seen} on seen instruction wordings), whereas for RadKev-9B it did not (\V{r3_wd9_held} and \V{r3_wd9_seen} points). % [C17]
Removing the imaging findings reduced the accuracy of every system on the subspecialty question, so classifying an interpreted case, as posed in the benchmark, is less demanding than routing a study before it is read. % [C13]

Two results concern the answers in the training data. On the RadCases panel question, the residual option that no topic applies was the most frequent training answer (\V{r3_rc_train_none_pct}\%), and the specialized models selected it preferentially (accuracy \V{r3_rcp_raw_rk27}\% for RadKev-27B) until their answer distributions were corrected for the training answer frequencies (\V{r3_t_v3_27_rcpanel}\%, against \V{r3_t_stock27_rcpanel}\% for Kev-27B). % [C3]
On radiologist-annotated reports from another institution (RadGraph-XL), specialization changed the accuracy of the 27B model by \V{r3_rg_d} points (\V{r3_rg_d_ci}) and that of the 9B model by \V{r3_rg_d9} points (\V{r3_rg_d9_ci}). Before and after specialization, the decision models frequently classified hedged findings as present (correct in \V{r3_rgu_stock27}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_rgu_v3_27}\% for RadKev-27B, against \V{r3_rgu_qwen38}\% for Qwen3.8-27B); such findings formed \V{r3_rgu_share}\% of these questions but only \V{r3_rgu_train_share}\% of the status answers in the training data, and specialization did not correct this tendency. The gains on the benchmark therefore do not carry over unchanged to reports from another institution, and the training answers should cover the answer distribution of the intended use. % [C22]

Beyond accuracy, a decision model returns a probability for every admissible answer of every question in one pass, which permits automation of confident decisions and referral of uncertain ones \citep{geifman2017selective}: at a 5\% error budget, RadKev-27B could answer \V{r3_cal_v3_27_cov}\% of the human-labeled questions, compared with \V{r3_cal_stock27_cov}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_cal_qwen38_cov}\% for Qwen3.8-27B. Its expected calibration error was similar to that of Kev-27B, but it made more confident errors (\V{r3_cal_v3_27_ce}\% vs \V{r3_cal_stock27_ce}\% of questions), which recalibration reduced (Section~\ref{sec:disc_clinical}). % [C11] [C12] [C15]
Per question, an LLM scored from its option-letter logits was approximately as fast as the decision model (\V{r3_lt_q} vs \V{r3_lt_rk27} ms), and with 255 options an LLM generating the number of its choice was faster (\V{r3_as_lat_q_255} vs \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_255} ms); the latency advantage applies to LLMs that generate their answers, particularly with reasoning (\V{r3_lt_qthink} s), and to records with several questions, which a decision model answers in one pass. % [C21] [C19]

These results agree with earlier work on domain adaptation, in which continued training on domain text improved downstream performance \citep{gururangan2020dontstop} and lightweight adaptation of open models has been proposed for clinical and radiology text \citep{vanveen2024adapted,huisman2025lightweight,bluethgen2025bestpractices,savage2025opensource}, although prompting alone has also allowed a generalist model to exceed medically fine-tuned models \citep{nori2023medprompt}. For decision models, which have been less accurate than frontier LLMs on medical questions (Section~\ref{sec:intro_spec}), the present results show that specialization can make an open model more accurate on radiology decisions than an LLM of the same size and than hosted general-purpose decision models. LLMs have been evaluated for the detection of errors in reports \citep{gertz2024errors} and for selecting imaging under the ACR Appropriateness Criteria \citep{rau2023accgpt,yao2025radcases}; in the present study, specialization on \V{rx_train} labeled reports improved error detection by \V{r3_prim_t_rexerr} points, whereas the assignment of ACR panels, with \V{rc_train} training cases, did not differ detectably from that of the starting model. % [C16]

\subsection{Clinical implementation}\label{sec:disc_clinical}

\paragraph{Decisions and infrastructure.} Table~\ref{tab:implementation} summarizes the recommendations below and the results on which they rest; none of these uses was evaluated prospectively. A decision model suits frequent decisions on radiology text whose admissible answers can be listed in advance, such as examination selection, prioritization, subspecialty routing and the detection of critical findings or report errors \citep{paschali2025foundation,bluethgen2025bestpractices}. Each decision should be validated on the information available when it is made: removing the imaging findings reduced subspecialty accuracy by \V{r3_pr_minchg} to \V{r3_pr_maxchg} points. The 27B model runs on two 48 GB GPUs and the 9B model on one (task mean \V{r3_a_v3_27_tm}\% vs \V{r3_a_v3_9_tm}\%), specialization took \V{r3_hours_27} h and \V{r3_hours_9} h on four such GPUs, and all decisions about a record should be posed in one request; unlike with a hosted decision model, patient data need not leave the institution. % [C13]

\paragraph{Training data.} The gains were largest where the training data contained the target decision (\V{r3_prim_trained_tm} vs \V{r3_prim_evalonly_tm} points), so the model should be specialized on labeled local examples of each decision, with answer frequencies that match local use: the residual option, \V{r3_rc_train_none_pct}\% of the RadCases training answers, became the preferred answer, and hedged findings, \V{r3_rgu_train_share}\% of the training status answers but \V{r3_rgu_share}\% of the external questions, remained the principal source of error. Questions should be posed with the wordings used in training (gain \V{r3_wd_held} vs \V{r3_wd_seen} points on withheld vs seen wordings). % [C3] [C17] [C22]

\paragraph{Answer spaces.} The model can choose only among the options offered, so the inclusion rate, the proportion of local cases whose correct answer is offered, should be measured first. Options can be taken from institutional catalogs, triage scales or reporting templates or, for open decisions such as differential diagnosis, from a curated catalog or an LLM. The similarity of the alternatives mattered more than their number (\V{r3_as_rk27_dx_rand255}\% vs \V{r3_as_rk27_dx_sim255}\% with 255 random vs most similar alternatives), so options should be mutually exclusive, synonyms merged and candidate lists validated in the form used. Options written together with the correct answer can reveal it (\V{r3_bl_dx_rk27}\% accuracy from the options alone), so answer spaces should be built independently of the correct answer and checked with an options-only control. The latency of RadKev-27B rose from \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_2} to \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_255} ms between 2 and 255 options, whereas that of the hosted decision models changed little, so a locally run model is best used with lists of tens of options. % [C18] [C21]

\paragraph{Calibration and monitoring.} Temperatures and confidence thresholds should be fitted on local data for each decision and answer space: with highly similar alternatives, mean confidence exceeded accuracy by \V{r3_asc_gap_sim255} points, although answers given with a confidence of at least 0.9 remained correct in at least \V{r3_asc_hi_lo}\% of questions. At an error rate of 5\%, \V{r3_cal_v3_27_cov}\% of the human-labeled questions could be automated and the remainder referred to a radiologist. In operation, predicted-answer frequencies should be compared with local expectations, which would have revealed the preference for the residual option (\V{r3_rc_none_panel_rk27}\% of panel questions) without labels, alongside periodic audits against radiologists. % [C12] [C25]

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The findings should be interpreted within the scope of the study design. The evaluation used public data: the test splits of Eurorad, RadCases, ReXErr, MedMCQA and MedQA derive from the same collections as their training splits, the errors in ReXErr reports were introduced by an LLM, and generalization to reports from another institution was assessed on one external test set (RadGraph-XL). Validation on institutional reports and across patient subgroups remains to be performed. Several tasks contain few questions, RSNA-RadioQA \V{r3_n_rsna} and some knowledge tasks fewer after the radiology filter, so their individual estimates are less precise, whereas averages over questions are dominated by the IU/Open-i chest radiograph reports; the primary outcome, the task mean, weights every task equally. The questions on imaging orders, triage and follow-up were labeled by the agreement of two LLMs and were used only to augment the training data. The LLM comparator, Qwen3.8-27B, was evaluated zero-shot with a single prompt and was not fine-tuned on the same data, so the contributions of the decision format and of domain fine-tuning are not separated. Each model was trained once, with a single random seed. Public examination questions and Eurorad cases may have been part of the pretraining data; because Qwen3.8-27B, Kev-27B and RadKev-27B share the same backbone, such exposure is common to the principal comparisons. Latency was measured with one request at a time, and batched serving may increase the throughput of every system. All questions were posed on text.

\subsection{Future work}\label{sec:future}

The specialized model should be compared with radiologists in a reader study on institutional reports, with answer spaces taken from the institution's own catalogs, and its error-detection and appropriateness decisions tested on reports and orders from other institutions. An LLM fine-tuned on the same data would separate the effect of the decision format from that of fine-tuning, and repeated training with different seeds would quantify the variability of the gains. Methods of constructing answer spaces should be compared by their inclusion rate and the accuracy and calibration they yield, routing should be evaluated on the information available before the read, and extending the approach to images would address the larger part of radiological practice.
