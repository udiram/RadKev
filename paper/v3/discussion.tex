\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Specializing a general-purpose decision model on public radiology data improved its task-averaged accuracy on a radiology benchmark by \V{r3_prim_tm} percentage points, and the specialized 27B model was more accurate than its starting point, the hosted OpenAI Decisions API, MedGemma-27B-text and its own backbone used as an LLM, over the benchmark as a whole and over the human-labeled tasks alone. It was also more accurate than that backbone with reasoning enabled, at \V{r3_lt_ratio_think}-fold lower latency. % [C1] [C4] [C5] [C16] [C19] [C23]
Before specialization, the backbone was approximately as accurate when queried as a general-purpose decision model as when queried as an LLM, so the advantage arose from specialization rather than from the decision format alone. % [C6]
At 9B, specialization raised the task mean by \V{r3_spec9_tm} points, and the specialized model did not differ detectably from a general-purpose model three times its size; at 27B, the gain from specialization did not differ detectably from that from a threefold increase in model size when tasks were weighted equally, and exceeded it when the human-labeled questions were pooled. % [C8] [C9]
Analyses other than the primary comparison are exploratory.

The gains were concentrated in a few tasks: they were largest in the detection of errors in reports and in Eurorad diagnosis, absent in finding presence and subspecialty classification, and larger on tasks from sources whose training splits were used for specialization (\V{r3_prim_trained_tm} points) than on tasks from sources used only for evaluation (\V{r3_prim_evalonly_tm} points). The options-only control qualifies the Eurorad gain: without the case, the specialized models still selected the correct diagnosis in most questions, and their gain over the general-purpose models was larger than with the case, so this gain reflects recognition of the case author's final diagnosis among the options rather than reading of the case. The same caution applies to other benchmarks whose incorrect options are written by the authors of the correct answer. % [C18]
For RadKev-27B, part of the gain also depended on familiar question phrasing (\V{r3_wd_held} points on withheld and \V{r3_wd_seen} on seen instruction wordings), whereas for RadKev-9B it did not (\V{r3_wd9_held} and \V{r3_wd9_seen} points). % [C17]
Removing the imaging findings reduced the accuracy of every system on the subspecialty question, so classifying an interpreted case, as posed in the benchmark, is less demanding than routing a study before it is read. % [C13]

Two results concern the answers in the training data. On the RadCases panel question, the residual option that no topic applies was the most frequent training answer (\V{r3_rc_train_none_pct}\%), and the specialized models selected it preferentially (accuracy \V{r3_rcp_raw_rk27}\% for RadKev-27B) until their answer distributions were corrected for the training answer frequencies (\V{r3_t_v3_27_rcpanel}\%, against \V{r3_t_stock27_rcpanel}\% for Kev-27B). % [C3]
On radiologist-annotated reports from another institution (RadGraph-XL), specialization changed the accuracy of the 27B model by \V{r3_rg_d} points (\V{r3_rg_d_ci}) and that of the 9B model by \V{r3_rg_d9} points (\V{r3_rg_d9_ci}). Before and after specialization, the decision models frequently classified hedged findings as present (correct in \V{r3_rgu_stock27}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_rgu_v3_27}\% for RadKev-27B, against \V{r3_rgu_qwen38}\% for Qwen3.8-27B); such findings formed \V{r3_rgu_share}\% of these questions but only \V{r3_rgu_train_share}\% of the status answers in the training data, and specialization did not correct this tendency. The gains on the benchmark therefore do not carry over unchanged to reports from another institution, and the training answers should cover the answer distribution of the intended use. % [C22]

Beyond accuracy, a decision model returns a probability for every admissible answer of every question in one pass, which permits automation of confident decisions and referral of uncertain ones \citep{geifman2017selective}: at a 5\% error budget, RadKev-27B could answer \V{r3_cal_v3_27_cov}\% of the human-labeled questions, compared with \V{r3_cal_stock27_cov}\% for Kev-27B and \V{r3_cal_qwen38_cov}\% for Qwen3.8-27B. Its expected calibration error was similar to that of Kev-27B, but it made more confident errors (\V{r3_cal_v3_27_ce}\% vs \V{r3_cal_stock27_ce}\% of questions), which recalibration reduced (Section~\ref{sec:disc_clinical}). % [C11] [C12] [C15]
Per question, an LLM scored from its option-letter logits was approximately as fast as the decision model (\V{r3_lt_q} vs \V{r3_lt_rk27} ms), and with 255 options an LLM generating the number of its choice was faster (\V{r3_as_lat_q_255} vs \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_255} ms); the latency advantage applies to LLMs that generate their answers, particularly with reasoning (\V{r3_lt_qthink} s), and to records with several questions, which a decision model answers in one pass. % [C21] [C19]

These results agree with earlier work on domain adaptation, in which continued training on domain text improved downstream performance \citep{gururangan2020dontstop} and lightweight adaptation of open models has been proposed for clinical and radiology text \citep{vanveen2024adapted,huisman2025lightweight,bluethgen2025bestpractices,savage2025opensource}, although prompting alone has also allowed a generalist model to exceed medically fine-tuned models \citep{nori2023medprompt}. For decision models, which have been less accurate than frontier LLMs on medical questions (Section~\ref{sec:intro_spec}), the present results show that specialization can make an open model more accurate than LLMs of the same size on radiology decisions. LLMs have been evaluated for the detection of errors in reports \citep{gertz2024errors} and for selecting imaging under the ACR Appropriateness Criteria \citep{rau2023accgpt,yao2025radcases}; specialization on \V{rx_train} labeled reports improved error detection substantially. % [C5] [C16]

\subsection{Clinical implementation}\label{sec:disc_clinical}

The results translate into practical recommendations for institutions that adopt a specialized decision model; Table~\ref{tab:implementation} summarizes them together with the results on which they rest. None of these uses was evaluated prospectively in this study.

\paragraph{Choice of decisions.} A decision model addresses the categorical decisions among the roles proposed for language models in radiology \citep{paschali2025foundation,bluethgen2025bestpractices} and complements, rather than replaces, generative systems. It suits frequent decisions made on radiology text whose admissible answers can be listed in advance: examination selection from an order's indication, prioritization of requests and worklists, routing to subspecialty readers, detection of critical or incidental findings and of report errors, extraction of follow-up recommendations, and consistency checks between findings and impression. It is less suited to decisions whose answers cannot be listed in advance, to generating text and to decisions that depend on the images. Each decision should be evaluated on the information available when it is made: the accuracy of every system on the subspecialty question fell by \V{r3_pr_minchg} to \V{r3_pr_maxchg} percentage points when the imaging findings were removed, so validation on interpreted cases overstates accuracy for routing before the read. % [C13]

\paragraph{Model and infrastructure.} All components are openly available and run on institutional hardware, so patient data need not leave the institution; the RadKev weights are licensed for non-commercial research use. A hosted decision model is inexpensive to query, as the benchmark evaluation of the OpenAI Decisions API, which cost US\$\V{r3_od_cost}, illustrates, but every request sends the report text outside the institution, and RadKev-27B was the more accurate of the two on the benchmark (\V{r3_od_tm} points). The 27B model requires two 48 GB GPUs and the 9B model one, at a moderate loss of accuracy (task mean \V{r3_a_v3_9_tm}\% vs \V{r3_a_v3_27_tm}\%); specialization took \V{r3_hours_27} h and \V{r3_hours_9} h on four such GPUs, so retraining on local data is feasible on a single workstation. Because a decision model answers all questions about a record in one pass, the decisions required for one report or order should be posed together in a single request. At the measured latencies (\V{r3_lt_rk27} and \V{r3_lt_rk9} ms per question), one GPU pair answers approximately \V{r3_thr_rk27_hr} questions per hour without batching (\V{r3_thr_rk9_hr} for the 9B model on one GPU), an extrapolation that assumes uninterrupted sequential requests. Specialization did not detectably reduce accuracy on Kev's out-of-domain transfer suite (\V{r3_tr_27} points at 27B and \V{r3_tr_9} points at 9B), so one specialized model can serve several kinds of decisions. % [C23]

\paragraph{Local training data.} The gains were largest where the training data contained the target decision: \V{r3_prim_trained_tm} points on tasks from sources whose training splits were used, against \V{r3_prim_evalonly_tm} points on tasks from sources used only for evaluation, and \V{r3_prim_t_rexerr} points in error detection after training on \V{rx_train} labeled reports. An institution should therefore specialize the model on labeled local examples of each decision that it intends to automate rather than rely on transfer from other radiology data. The answers of these examples should follow the local answer distribution. The residual option, which formed \V{r3_rc_train_none_pct}\% of the RadCases training answers, became the preferred answer of the specialized models, and hedged findings, which formed \V{r3_rgu_train_share}\% of the status answers in training but \V{r3_rgu_share}\% of the external test questions, remained the principal source of error on reports from another institution; rare but clinically important answers, such as equivocal findings, should therefore be represented deliberately. Questions should be posed in deployment with the wordings used in training, preferably as a fixed template for each decision, because the gain of RadKev-27B was smaller on withheld than on seen wordings (\V{r3_wd_held} vs \V{r3_wd_seen} points). % [C3] [C17] [C22]

\paragraph{Answer spaces.} A decision model can only choose among the answers it is offered, so the answer space defines what the model can decide. Six properties of the answer space bear on implementation.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{Completeness.} The correct answer must be among the options. The inclusion rate, the proportion of local cases whose correct answer is in the answer space, bounds the achievable accuracy and should be measured before deployment. A residual option, such as ``none of the above'' or ``other'', gives cases outside the list a defined answer, but its frequency among the training answers determines how often a fine-tuned model selects it (Section~\ref{sec:res_primary}).
  \item \emph{Source.} For closed decisions, such as the examination, protocol, priority or follow-up category, the answer space can be taken from the institution's own catalogs, triage scales or structured-reporting templates, which are reviewed, versioned and audited. For open decisions such as differential diagnosis, candidates can be retrieved from a curated catalog by similarity to the case or written by an LLM, and the decision model used to choose among them with a calibrated probability.
  \item \emph{Composition.} Accuracy depended more on how similar the alternatives were to the correct answer than on their number: with 255 options, RadKev-27B was correct in \V{r3_as_rk27_dx_rand255}\% of Eurorad questions with random and in \V{r3_as_rk27_dx_sim255}\% with the most similar alternatives (RSNA-RadioQA, \V{r3_as_rk27_rsna_rand255}\% and \V{r3_as_rk27_rsna_sim255}\%), and LLM-written alternatives were less confusable than the most similar diagnoses of the pool. Candidate lists retrieved by similarity therefore pose the most difficult decisions and should be validated in that form. Synonyms should be merged, and options should be mutually exclusive and at a consistent level of detail, because the model divides probability among options that describe the same answer.
  \item \emph{Independence from the correct answer.} The answer options alone allowed RadKev-27B to select the case author's diagnosis in \V{r3_bl_dx_rk27}\% of Eurorad questions, so answer spaces written together with the correct answer can reveal it. Answer spaces should be constructed independently of the correct answer, and an options-only control on local cases, in which accuracy should fall to chance, tests whether they are.
  \item \emph{Size.} Latency per request rose from \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_2} ms with two options to \V{r3_as_lat_rk27_255} ms with 255. Lists of tens of options limit latency, and a large catalog, such as the \V{rc_topics} ACR Appropriateness Criteria topics, can be narrowed by a coarser question, such as the panel, before the final choice.
  \item \emph{Calibration.} With the 255 most similar alternatives, the mean confidence of RadKev-27B exceeded its accuracy by \V{r3_asc_gap_sim255} points, whereas answers given with a confidence of at least 0.9 remained correct in at least \V{r3_asc_hi_lo}\% of questions; the proportion of questions that reached this confidence fell from \V{r3_asc_share_rand16}\% with 16 random to \V{r3_asc_share_sim255}\% with 255 most similar alternatives. Confidence thresholds therefore remain informative in confusable answer spaces, but the proportion of decisions that can be automated depends on the answer space, and temperatures and thresholds should be fitted for each answer space and refitted when a catalog changes. Where the answer frequencies of the training data differ from those expected locally, the answer distributions can be corrected for them without retraining \citep{saerens2002adjusting}, as for the RadCases panel question (accuracy \V{r3_rcp_raw_rk27}\% before and \V{r3_t_v3_27_rcpanel}\% after correction).
\end{enumerate}
% [C3] [C18] [C21] [C25]

\paragraph{Thresholds, review and monitoring.} Before deployment, the temperature should be refitted on a labeled local sample and a confidence threshold chosen for each decision at the error rate that the decision tolerates; on the benchmark, RadKev-27B could answer \V{r3_cal_v3_27_cov}\% of the human-labeled questions at an error rate of 5\%, and the remaining questions would be referred to a radiologist. RadKev-27B made more confident errors than Kev-27B as scored (\V{r3_cal_v3_27_ce}\% vs \V{r3_cal_stock27_ce}\% of questions), and recalibration on the test data reduced them to \V{r3_rc_v3_27_ce}\%, so a temperature fitted on development data should not be assumed to hold locally. In operation, the frequencies of the predicted answers should be compared with the expected local frequencies: a systematic preference, such as that for the residual option, which the specialized models selected for \V{r3_rc_none_panel_rk27}\% of the panel questions before correction, is visible in these frequencies without labels. Periodic audits against the decisions of radiologists, revalidation after changes to the model, the catalogs or the report templates, integration with the radiology information system and review by a radiologist of decisions with clinical consequences \citep{palmer2026kev} complete the safeguards. % [C3] [C12]

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The test splits of Eurorad, RadCases, ReXErr, MedMCQA and MedQA come from the same collections as their training splits, and ReXErr's errors were inserted by an LLM. Apart from the sources used only for evaluation, whose questions follow the formats of the training tasks, the only external test set was RadGraph-XL, on which specialization lowered the accuracy of the 27B model. The correction of the RadCases panel distributions for the training answer frequencies was chosen after the results were known. RSNA-RadioQA comprises \V{r3_n_rsna} questions and several knowledge tasks retain few questions after the radiology filter, so their estimates are imprecise, and the IU/Open-i chest radiograph reports dominate any average over questions. The questions on imaging orders, triage and follow-up were labeled by agreement of two LLMs without validation against human judgment \citep{pangakis2023validation}, and the subspecialty question, posed with the imaging findings, measures the classification of an interpreted case rather than routing. % [C13]
All data were public, the models were not validated on institutional reports, and accuracy was not examined across patient subgroups. The LLMs were evaluated zero-shot with a single prompt, MedGemma-27B-text without reasoning in the main evaluation, and no LLM was fine-tuned on the same data, so the effect of the decision format is not separated from that of domain fine-tuning. Every model was trained once, with a single random seed; public examination questions and Eurorad cases may have been part of the pretraining data of every model, so the conclusions rest on paired differences; and the analysis plan was not publicly registered. Latency was measured one request at a time, and LLMs served with batching may achieve higher throughput. All questions were posed on text.

\subsection{Future work}\label{sec:future}

The specialized model should be compared with radiologists in a reader study on institutional reports, with answer spaces taken from the institution's own catalogs, and its error-detection and appropriateness decisions tested on reports and orders from other institutions. An LLM fine-tuned on the same data would separate the effect of the decision format from that of fine-tuning, and repeated training with different seeds would quantify the variability of the gains. Methods of constructing answer spaces should be compared by their inclusion rate and the accuracy and calibration they yield, routing should be evaluated on the information available before the read, and extending the approach to images would address the larger part of radiological practice.
